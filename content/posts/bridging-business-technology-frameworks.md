Frameworks like TOGAF, FEAF, and Zachman have shaped how organizations think about enterprise architecture. But too many teams confuse **using a framework** with **doing architecture**.

## The Framework Trap

I've seen organizations spend months perfecting their ADM cycle documentation while the business made critical technology decisions without architectural input. The framework became the goal, and the actual purpose — guiding decisions — got lost.

## Frameworks as Starting Points

The best architects I've worked with treat frameworks like a musician treats sheet music: they know the theory deeply enough to improvise when the situation demands it.

Here's a healthier approach:

- **Learn the framework deeply** — understand why each phase exists
- **Adapt ruthlessly** — cut what doesn't add value in your context
- **Focus on communication** — the best architecture artifact is the one your stakeholder actually reads
- **Measure by influence** — did your framework help someone make a better decision?

## What Works in Practice

After years of applying architecture frameworks across logistics, facility management, and technology organizations, here's what I've found actually works:

1. **Lightweight capability maps** beat comprehensive catalogs
2. **Decision records** beat design documents
3. **Architecture principles with real examples** beat abstract governance policies
4. **Regular stakeholder conversations** beat quarterly review boards

## The Bottom Line

A framework should accelerate your thinking, not constrain it. If your framework makes you slower at delivering value, you're using it wrong.

> "The best architecture framework is the one your organization actually uses."

Start with the business problem. Let the framework serve you, not the other way around.
